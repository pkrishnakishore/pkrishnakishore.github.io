Research approach

Why work across
disciplines?

Complex questions often connect technical mechanisms, human behavior, and institutional decisions.

For me, working across disciplines means connecting methods to a shared question: using engineering to examine a system, policy analysis to understand its institutional setting, and data and synthesis to assess the evidence. My agritech, emissions-policy, and evaluation work makes those connections concrete.

The National Academies describes interdisciplinary research as integrating perspectives and methods to address questions beyond one field. A study by Uzzi and colleagues found that highly cited research often combines an established knowledge base with unusual connections. More recently, Wang and colleagues’ review of AI in science highlights the importance of data quality and stewardship alongside new computational methods.

My takeaway is to build on disciplinary depth while learning to collaborate across methods and sectors. In my own work, that means making assumptions explicit, checking the evidence, and communicating what a finding can support.

Selected reading

National Academies (2005), Facilitating Interdisciplinary Research, Chapter 2. A foundational account of integration across fields.

Uzzi, Mukherjee, Stringer & Jones (2013), “Atypical Combinations and Scientific Impact,” Science, 342, 468–472. Evidence on knowledge combinations and citation impact.

Wang and colleagues (2023), “Scientific discovery in the age of artificial intelligence,” Nature, 620, 47–60. A review of computational opportunities and challenges across scientific fields.

Developing research interests

Institutions, expertise,
and public decisions

Alongside my applied work, I am developing questions about how expertise, organizational incentives, and evidence shape decisions. Comparative politics is one direction within this broader research interest.

Political consultants and authority within parties

A proposed comparative study asks when consultants shift decision-making authority inside parties, and toward whom. The developing design considers comparative cases in India, document analysis, interviews, and process tracing. This is a proposed study.

Campaign expertise and grassroots party organization

A developing practitioner working paper based on a local election in Andhra Pradesh. It distinguishes firsthand observations from reported accounts and addresses my dual position as the candidate’s son and campaign strategist. It is separate from the proposed comparative doctoral study.

From political strategy to public spending

A developing project on electoral commitments, budgets, and implementation in India. Proposed sources include manifestos, budget documents, and interviews. No completed findings or public manuscript are claimed here.
